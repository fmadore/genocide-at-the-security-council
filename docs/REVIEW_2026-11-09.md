# Repository review — 9 November 2026

A pass over the pipeline's statistics, the numbers the dashboard computes and
draws, and the figures themselves, after the move to Sakamoto–Matsuoka v6.0
(1946–2026). Items already tracked in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) are not
repeated. Unlike the review of 8 October, most of what follows was changed on
the branch that carries this file; each item says whether it is **done**,
**for you to decide**, or **open**. The measurements were made on the pinned
corpus, rebuilt from `data/raw` on the review machine, and on the payload
published on 3 November.

**Overall.** The arithmetic is sound where it was checked: Wilson, the
Kitagawa decomposition, Gries's DP, logDice, the block-permutation rate test,
Cohen's kappa, PABAK, MASI and Krippendorff's alpha, the Hájek estimator and
trustworthiness all compute what they say. What was wrong came from two
places. One is the corpus migration: several rules were written for 1992–2023,
where every year and quarter held thousands of speeches, and were never asked
again of a record in which a year can hold 86. The other is three places where
a figure's sentence describes a stricter quantity than the one computed.

---

## A. Method — what was wrong, and what was done

**A1. Rates on denominators that cannot carry them (done).** Only the monthly
grid withheld a rate below 125 speeches. On 1946–2026, 1959 holds 86 speeches
in four meetings, 39 quarters hold fewer than 125 and three fewer than 30, and
a cell of the speaker-group split can be one speech. Their rates were published
and drawn like any other: `icj` peaked at 25.7% on 9 of 35 speeches (1954Q3),
and one non-state speech in 1993 set the split figure's axis to 100%, pressing
P5 and E10 into its bottom twentieth. One rule now at every grain
(`series_payload.MIN_SPEECHES_PER_PERIOD`): counts always, rates and both
intervals null below the minimum, `sufficient` beside them. The change-point
pass still reads the un-withheld frames, because it tests counts.

**A2. Eight quarters missing from the axis (done).** The quarterly series was
built from the quarters observed. 1953Q1–Q2, 1959Q2, 1959Q4, 1963Q1, 1967Q1,
2020Q2 and 2021Q1 hold no speech, so the chart joined 1952Q4 to 1953Q3, and
2020Q1 (2.09%, n = 1,197) to 2020Q3 (0.45%, n = 222). The series is now a
complete grid (`series.quarter_grid`): 322 quarters, 275 with a rate.

**A3. The meeting-clustered band claimed certainty where there was least (done).**
A percentile bootstrap returns only rates its resamples produce. Where no
meeting of a period used the word, every resample is zero and the interval is
0 to 0: 73 of 314 quarters and 7 of 81 years for `genocide`, 5,315 quarter
cells over the 28 terms. With one term-bearing meeting it is narrower than
Wilson again (1992Q2: Wilson 0.24–7.17%, drawn 0–2.70%). In 161 of 314
quarters the band the caption said "widens" was narrower than the interval it
replaced. The dashboard now draws the envelope of the two intervals
(`bandBounds`); the bootstrap's docstring says what it cannot do.

**A4. "Who stays silent" ignored who spoke first (done; the default is yours).**
The figure's question is whether a delegation uses the word "when another
speaker has just used it". The denominator counted every meeting in which
anyone else used it, in any order. In 10,456 of 30,752 such attendances the
delegation had finished speaking before anyone else said the word. The source
carries the speaking order, so `actors.exposure` now writes a second pair,
`exposed_after` and `joined_after`: meetings in which the delegation took the
floor after another's first use, and those in which it then used it. The
room's share is 13.4% on that reading against 11.3%; a delegation's share
moves by up to 10 points; 142 of 156 delegations keep a published share; the
ordering barely moves (Spearman 0.94). The figure reads the ordered counts and
the download carries both. If you prefer the old default, `silence.ordered` is
the one function to switch off.

**A5. The keyword tables ranked noise (done).** Tables were filtered by
G² ≥ 10.83 and ranked by log ratio with a half-count for a zero. Over the
17,662 candidate words of the main table a per-word threshold of p < 0.001
lets dozens through by chance, and that ranking puts exactly those first. In
*Compared with a like-for-like speech*, 82 of 100 rows never occurred in the
control set, 98 had DP above 0.95 and 36 occurred in five meetings or fewer;
the table opened with the selection word itself (7,055 against 0 by
construction) and continued with `punta`, `este`, `escudos`, `salazar's`,
`javanese`. Rows are now ranked by the conservative log ratio (Evert 2022;
`lexical.conservative_log_ratio`): the end, nearest 1, of an exact
Clopper–Pearson interval on the rate ratio at 0.05 divided by the number of
words tested, and zero when the interval contains 1. Seven rows of 100 now
have an empty control and three sit in five meetings or fewer; the table
reads *tutsi*, *interahamwe*, *humanity*, *cleansing*, *crimes*, *rwanda*,
*atrocity*, *srebrenica*, *yazidi*, with the Kashmir debates of 1948–71
(*punjab*, *sikhs*, *jammu*) beside them. The delegation tables follow the
same rule: zero-control rows fall from 3,580 of 5,880 to 392 of 5,354; all
147 delegations keep a table, 38 of them shorter than forty rows, the
shortest six. The selection word's own forms are marked with a dagger, not
removed. Two things this does not settle: a word concentrated in one long
debate can still rank (`sikhs`, 152 occurrences in 11 meetings), which is
what the dispersion columns beside it are for; and `genocide's` and
`post-genocide` are not marked, because only a full match of the pattern is.

**A6. The matching summary scored a missing word as zero (done).** "Pairing
reduced the largest differences by 8.53" was, for Russia, the median unmatched
log ratio: all fifteen words had left the 40-row matched table and were scored
as a fall to parity. 119 of 147 speakers had eight or more of fifteen absent.
The sentence now counts the words that left and gives a median only over words
in both tables, withheld below five.

**A7. A part-year drawn as a year (done).** The record ends on 23 June 2026.
The overview compared "the final year" (3,253 speeches) with the first, the
count charts drew 2026 as a collapse (157 occurrences against 589), and the
month grid marked July–December 2026 as months in which "the Council held no
speeches". The series now publish `record.first_date` and `last_date`; the
overview outlines the part-year and compares the last whole year; the grid has
a fourth state for months outside the record.

**A8. The semantic map's fidelity figure measures the wrong scale (done for
the next release).** Trustworthiness and "neighbours lost" are computed inside
a 1,000-speech subsample. Among 1,000 of 182,684 speeches, a speech's 25
nearest are its nearest 2.5% of the corpus, some 4,500 speeches in the full
map: the figure describes broad layout, not which points sit side by side.
`projection.local_fidelity` finds each query's neighbours among every point,
exactly, and step 21 writes it as `evaluation.local`; with every point a query
it equals `trustworthiness`, which a test holds. The page words the old figure
as what it is and shows the new one when a release carries it. It needs the
embeddings, so it waits on the cluster (ROADMAP item 8). CLAIMS and PLAN also
quoted the diagnostics of the release before v6.0; corrected.

**A9. Smaller, all done.** The profile table printed a period the figure
refuses (14 speeches against a minimum of 20). The agenda list showed five
items of the eight its remainder row excludes, so Russia's list summed to
93.3%. A usage cell of 3 in 1,094 printed "0%". A rare term's axis read
0.0%, 0.0%, 0.1%, 0.1%. A CSV column named `corpus_tokens` held words; it is
`corpus_words`.

## B. For you to decide

**B1. The coder packet is not mixed, and so not quite blind.** `packet()`
orders by `_rank(occurrence_id, seed)`, the hash the probability frame is
drawn with. The 120 lowest hashes of the corpus are therefore the first 120
positions of the packet, exactly, and every one of the 338 after them was put
there by a model label or a cue: 259 by the published run's `rejects`,
`conditional`, `reports_without_position`, `other` or pre-onset label. A coder
who knows the design, and one of the two wrote it, knows from position alone
which kind of passage is on the screen. The docstring says the order "mixes
the frames". Either shuffle with a seed of its own, or keep
probability-frame-first as a stated choice (ROADMAP item 1 wants it coded
first) and say in the evaluation plan that position reveals the frame. Not
changed: it reorders a packet you may already have opened.

**B2. Two of the three frames share a hash, so they are not independent.**
`draw()` uses seeds 21, 22 and 23. The coverage frame's random fill ranks by
its seed plus one, which is 23, the third frame's own. An occurrence low in
that ranking tends into both, and `union_inclusion`'s `1 − Π(1 − p)` assumes
it does not: 8 occurrences sit in both frames against 4.7 expected. The fill
is small (43 draws of 8,574), so `pi_union` is overstated by at most about
half a point and a weight by at most about 5%, for the 269 model-stratified
units only. Nothing is coded yet, so the clean fix is free today: give the
fill its own seed and redraw. It changes which 43 passages the fill holds.

**B3. The `rejects` test counts occurrences as independent.** 235 `rejects`
occurrences sit in 154 speeches; 12 of Sudan's 25 are one speech, 8 of
Yugoslavia's 32 another; 58% of multi-occurrence speeches carry one position
throughout. The exact binomial test and the Wilson interval in
`usage.position_rows` treat each as a trial. The markers are hidden until the
gold sample measures `rejects` (RV36), so nothing published is wrong, but when
they return the unit should be the speech (a speech rejects if any of its
occurrences does) or the test should resample speeches. The change-point test
made the same move from speeches to meetings (RV19).

**B4. New wording on the site.** Every fix that changed a sentence, and both
new figures, carry text you have not approved, and these commits do not carry
your `Wording:` prefix. The places: the band's caveat and the withheld-period
and part-record caveats under *The word list over time*; the caveat of *Who
says it, and in what debate*; the overview's sentence on speech volume; the
reading note and rule of *Who stays silent*; the matching sentence and the
conservative-estimate notes on both keyword figures, with a new Methods
glossary entry; the fidelity sentence under the semantic map; the two-interval
paragraph on Methods; "more ink" for "darker" in three reading notes; and all
the text of *Agenda or usage?* and of the rate-split drawing.

## C. Figures

**C1. Two new figures on the Chronology (done).** *Agenda or usage?* draws
`series/decomposition.json`, until now a closed table with one of its two
splits unused: per pair of decades, the composition and within parts as
diverging bars, the net change as a tick, the largest groups in text, rows on
fewer than 100 term-bearing speeches muted. *Testing for a change in the rate*
gains the two segment rates as steps with their intervals, each year's rate
behind them, and an unaccepted split drawn dashed.

**C2. Visual defects (done; listed in the commit).** Found from screenshots
of every route at two widths in both themes. The ones that misled rather than
merely looked wrong: the split figure's axis (A1); a bar rule striking through
the pooled-month rates so that 1.80% read as .80%; register hues, which are
claims about vocabulary, used for over- and under-use in the delegation
keyword figure; reading notes that say "darker is stronger" where the dark
theme draws the strongest cell lightest; interval strips scaled to the table's
largest bound, leaving most intervals ten pixels wide. One cause sat under
several: ECharts' label-layout feature was never registered in
`web/src/lib/echarts.ts`, so every `moveOverlap` and `hideOverlap` on the
site had done nothing, and end labels printed over one another.

**C4. Open on existing figures.** The overview's count and share still share
one plot on two axes, so where the bars meet the line is an accident of the
two scales; two stacked panels on one time axis would remove it. *When each
delegation first said it* runs its axis to the curve's own maximum, so the
curve appears to saturate; the risk set (`exposed`) is the honest ceiling.
*Naming a people, or not* gives a decade of 36 occurrences the same bar as one
of 2,820.

## D. Refactoring

Nothing found that pays for itself now; the review of 8 October took the
large ones. Three small things: `export_web.py --update-contract` rewrites the
contract from whatever is in `web/static/data`, including a payload the export
just refused, and narrows every deliberately wide type (`float|null`, `[]`)
to what one payload happened to hold, so it wants a merge mode or a warning.
`04_series.py` still imports names from `lib.series_payload` only to re-export
them. `lib.usage` re-exports forty names for the same reason.

## E. Checked and found correct

Wilson and its clipping; `zero_ceiling` and the minimum derived from it; the
Kitagawa parts adding to the change; the block null reproducing the counts it
tests; Bonferroni over the planned rate tests; matched controls drawn without
replacement per stratum, with the shortfall reported; DP by the sparse sum;
logDice; nPMI and the suppressed definitional pairs; kappa withheld on a flat
margin; PABAK on declared categories; MASI and alpha against the pairwise
form; the Hájek estimator and the difference estimator for corrected shares;
Benjamini–Hochberg; trustworthiness against Venna and Kaski's formula. On the
dashboard: no nonzero rate printed as zero, no withheld value exported as
zero, rankings confined to sufficient rows, matrix rows summing to their
margins, stability ranges containing the printed value in 5,880 of 5,880 rows.

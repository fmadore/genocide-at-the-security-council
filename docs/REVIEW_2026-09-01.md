# Review: methodology, analysis and dashboard

Status: 1 September 2026, read at commit `c5ad080`. A read-only assessment of the whole
repository: the pipeline and its statistics, the lexicon, the model-assisted layer and its
prompt, the dashboard's figures and prose, the non-adopted methods, the infrastructure and
the documentation. It supplements `IMPROVEMENT_ROADMAP.md` in the way that document
supplemented the assessment of `752ef66`; nothing here is a decision.

How it was done. Every script under `scripts/`, every route and figure component under
`web/src/`, the four `docs/*.md`, the codebook, the prompt and the four committed model runs
were read. `python -m pytest` and `ruff check .` pass on a fresh Python 3.11 environment.
The corpus is not in the checkout, so no pipeline step was re-run; every number quoted about
the model runs was recomputed from the committed `annotations.jsonl` files and reproduces
`VALIDATION.md` §7. Line references are to the files at `c5ad080`. Word counts for the
dashboard are approximate: text nodes and figure props, every conditional branch counted.

## 1. Verdict

The project is unusually well built. The corpus is pinned by Harvard's own checksums and
verified offline; every artefact carries its inputs, configuration and commit; the payload's
shape is a tested contract at the export seam; every figure on the site states its
question, its reading, its caveat and its source; and every decision is written down with
its date and its argument. Very few digital-humanities repositories reach this standard,
and none of the findings below is a reason to lower it.

The problems are of three kinds, and they are ranked here by how much they could move a
published figure.

1. **The inferential statistics assume an independence the corpus does not have.** The
   change-point test, the keyness tables and the collocate rankings all treat speeches or
   tokens as independent draws, while speeches are nested in meetings whose agenda decides
   whether *genocide* is sayable at all. The p-values are therefore anti-conservative, the
   "20-seed band" is not a confidence interval, and no interval of any kind is drawn on the
   site. The fix is one the roadmap already names (S1, meeting-block bootstrap) and it is
   the most important analytical change available.
2. **Two small bugs in the lexicon machinery, and several terms that measure something
   other than genocide talk.** Prefilters are stricter than the regexes they guard, so a
   phrase broken across a line is never counted; register roll-ups double-count nested
   terms. `survivors`, `commemoration`, `denial`, `glorification` and `holocaust` will
   mostly track other agendas.
3. **The site says too much, and much of it is the wrong kind of saying.** About 12,500
   visible words over seven views for twenty figures, 5,200 of them figure apparatus,
   with the change-point test explained three times, one 26-word download hint printed
   eighteen times, and a good deal of narration about the pipeline's own virtue that a
   reader cannot act on. The author's suspicion is right.

On the model-assisted layer: the prompt is a good instrument and the two full runs are in
better shape than "experimental" implies for the named-case referents and for rejections,
and worse for the report/assert boundary, which is where 445 of the 1,148 stance
disagreements sit. The gold sample as designed (200 occurrences) will estimate overall
agreement and nothing per class; the two runs make a better design possible now.

## 2. What to keep

These are the things the review would defend against any simplification.

- **Rates before counts, with the raw count kept as the contrast.** The home page's first
  figure is the right first figure.
- **Withholding below a declared minimum, and hatching it rather than blanking it.** The
  argument for the rule is wrong in one respect (§3.3), the practice is right.
- **Matched controls for keyness**, with the unmatched comparison published beside them as
  the thing the matching improves on.
- **The contract at the export seam**, and the rule that a figure's arithmetic lives in a
  tested module and the component only renders.
- **The human/model separation**: `annotations/` never written by a script,
  `model_annotations/` never merged, `current_run.txt` as a reviewed diff.
- **The prompt's task boundary** and its verbatim-quote rule, which works.
- **The refusal to adopt topic models** on the evidence, and the evidence itself.
- **The decision record.** The cost of it is discussed in §7; its existence is not the
  problem.

## 3. Statistical methods

The methods are documented with unusual care, and the two central design decisions (rates
rather than counts; matched controls rather than a whole-corpus reference) are right. The
findings below are about what the inferential claims rest on, ranked by how much they could
move a published figure.

### 3.1 Change points: the null model ignores the corpus's structure

`lib/series.py:597-665` scans every single split of an annual series, computes the
likelihood-ratio gain under a binomial (speech prevalence) or Poisson (token rate) model, and
calibrates it by simulating 2,000 no-change series with the observed exposures. The
construction is clean and the Phipson–Smyth `+1` (`:637-645`) is correct. Three problems:

- **Speeches are treated as independent trials.** They are nested in meetings whose agenda
  fixes whether *genocide* is sayable at all: S/PV.7155 alone holds 198 occurrences
  (`docs/CORPUS.md` §8.6). The binomial variance of an annual share at n ≈ 4,800 and
  p ≈ 0.03 is about 6 × 10⁻⁶; the between-meeting component will dwarf it. The simulated
  maxima are therefore far too small and `accepted` will be true for any visible bump. The
  caveat text says so (`04_series.py:551-560`); the model does not. `denominators()` already
  computes `meetings` per period (`series.py:121`) and never uses it. The minimum fix is a
  meeting-block bootstrap (roadmap S1) or a beta-binomial / negative-binomial null with
  dispersion estimated from meeting-level residuals.
- **One break is the wrong model for a bump-shaped series.** The genocide rate has two
  humps (1993–96 and 2013–15). The best single split of a hump lands on one of its edges and
  reports before/after means pooled over heterogeneous regimes; the 2017 split with a
  ratio of 0.71 in the README is that artefact. A segmented (piecewise-linear) quasi-binomial
  GLM, or a GAM `rate ~ s(year)` with meeting-clustered errors, would separate a level shift
  from drift; PELT with a BIC penalty would give the number of breaks rather than one date.
- **Bonferroni is applied to the wrong family.** The three corrected tests (`04_series.py:523`)
  run on nested, nearly identical data, so the correction is conservative where it matters
  least. The multiplicity that matters is the *uncorrected* exploratory block: eight series ×
  up to four greedy WBS tests (`series.py:489-523`), which is what the findings note narrates.

Two smaller points. The WBS permutation (`series.py:427-449`) permutes annual rates without
weights, so a year resting on 1,023 speeches is exchangeable with one resting on 7,621; and
the findings note in `notes/` is built from the exploratory `change["series"]` block rather
than the corrected `inference` block (`04_series.py:696-732`), with hard-coded sentences
("after 2013", "the 2014 spike", "remains 1994" at `:700-707`; "The elevated months are not
the commemorative ones" unconditionally at `:604`) that would stay true in prose whatever the
data said. The notes are not published, but they are what the author reads.

### 3.2 Keyness and collocates: significance without dispersion

`lib/lexical.py:122-157` implements G² and Hardie's log ratio correctly, and the
year × agenda × speaker-group matching (`:267-327`) is a sound design that the site explains
well. What is missing is the corpus-linguistic standard of the last decade:

- **No dispersion per row.** G² on pooled token counts assumes tokens are independent; a
  collocate repeated fifty times in one speech ranks as if it appeared in fifty speeches.
  Nothing carries document frequency, distinct meetings, Gries's DP or Juilland's D beside a
  keyword. Roadmap S5 names this; today `compare()` sorts on `g2` (`:191`), the least
  defensible column.
- **The 20-seed band is a sensitivity range, not a confidence interval.** It captures
  control-draw variance only; target sampling variance and meeting clustering are outside
  it. A meeting-block bootstrap would give an interval that means what a reader assumes.
- **Scare-quoted *genocide* is invisible.** `TOKEN_RE` (`lexical.py:43`) keeps trailing
  apostrophes, so `'genocide'` tokenises as `genocide'`, a separate type. The one usage this
  study most wants to see (the contested, distanced use) drops out of every keyness table.
  `R2P` becomes `r`, `p`; `U.S.` becomes `u`, `s`.
- **The PMI network measures topical co-presence, not lexical association.** Speech-level
  co-occurrence with a 20-speech floor and nPMI is fine for what it is, but `denial`'s pattern
  contains `genocid` (`config/lexicon.yml:145`), so the `denial–genocide` edge is partly
  definitional and is not listed under `suppressed_nested_edges`.

### 3.3 Denominators and withholding

- **The token rate divides body-only regex counts by the codebook's `tokens` column**
  (`series.py:160`, `01_build_parquet.py:170`). That column is quanteda's count over the full
  text, punctuation and numbers included: 66.4 M against the 59 M `lib/lexical.py` counts as
  words. Every "per 100,000 words" figure is therefore about 11 % low relative to the
  language page's universe, and the label "words" is not what was counted. Either count
  words once and use that everywhere, or label the unit "tokens (codebook)".
- **The 100-speech rule licenses only the interpretation of zeros.** The derivation
  (P(0 hits | n, p = 0.031) < 0.05 ⇒ n ≥ 96) says nothing about a speaker with 60 speeches
  and 12 hits, whose Wilson interval [11 %, 32 %] is informative. Withholding turns a
  gradient into a cliff. Publishing Wilson or Jeffreys intervals beside every rate, and a
  funnel plot (roadmap S3), would let the reader see volatility instead of an absence. The
  ranking by speech rate in `11_countries.py:409-417` is exposed to winner's curse at
  n ≈ 100.

### 3.4 Lexicon: two bugs and several noisy terms

- **Prefilters are stricter than the regexes they guard.** `Term.count`
  (`lib/lexicon.py:48-62`) runs the regex only on speeches containing a literal prefilter.
  `war_crimes` has prefilter `war crime` but regex `\bwar\s+crimes?\b`; `\s+` matches a
  newline, the literal does not, and the record's hard line breaks are preserved
  (`lib/text.py:39` normalises CRLF only). The same applies to `mass_atrocity`,
  `never_again`, `early_warning`, `responsibility_to_protect`, `genocide_convention` and
  `prevention_of_genocide`. Whether it matters depends on how often a phrase straddles a
  line break; measure it with one pass (regex hits on rows the prefilter rejected) and either
  assert `regex ⇒ prefilter` in `tests/test_config.py` or drop prefilters for multi-word
  terms. `08_kwic.py`'s reconciliation cannot catch this, because it compares two outputs of
  the same filter.
- **Register and total roll-ups double-count nested terms.** `lexicon.apply`
  (`lib/lexicon.py:176-187`) sums `n_<term>` per register and into `n_lexicon_total`.
  `mass_atrocity` is nested under `atrocity` and both are `legal`, so the legal register's
  occurrence count and token rate (which the chronology publishes, `series.py:50-56`)
  count every *mass atrocity* twice; `n_lexicon_total` double-counts all four nested pairs.
  The `has_` flags used for speech rates are unaffected.
- **Noisy adjacent terms.** `survivors` (survivors of sexual violence; the WPS agenda will
  drive that series), `commemoration` (`anniversar\w+` catches anniversaries of resolution
  1325 and of independence), `denial` (`\bdenial\b` alone matches "denial of humanitarian
  access", a stock phrase; `deny\w*` misses *denies* and *denied*), `glorification`
  (glorification of terrorism per S/RES/1624), `holocaust` (`nuclear holocaust`),
  `ethnic_hatred` (mostly `ethnic conflict`, misnamed), `tribunals` (matches the Tribunal for
  the Law of the Sea, misses the Residual Mechanism, so the accountability register decays
  after 2015 for institutional reasons). The commemorative register as built tracks UN
  anniversaries, not genocide memory; an anchored variant (co-occurrence with `genocid*`
  within the sentence) is the cheap remedy, and the `denial` pattern already gestures at it.
- **Construct validity of `\bgenocid\w*`.** It folds `genocidaire(s)` (an actor label for
  ex-FAR/Interahamwe in DRC debates) into the count of the word as event qualification. The
  two model runs disagree on exactly this (Luna calls 4 of 29 false positives; Gemini none).
  Give `genocidaires` its own term.
- **Missing terms** a reader will ask about: `massacre(s)` and `mass killing(s)` (the
  pre-qualification vocabulary whose replacement by *genocide* is the study's object),
  `ICJ` / `International Court of Justice` (four of the 35 reference dates are ICJ
  filings), `intent to destroy` / `in whole or in part` (the Convention's definitional
  phrase), `incitement` (Article III), `crime of crimes`, `persecution`. Case names
  (`Srebrenica`, `Tutsi`, `Yazidi`, `Rohingya`) belong in a referent lexicon, not in term
  counts.
- **Housekeeping.** The header of `config/lexicon.yml` says "four discursive families"; there
  are six register values. `core` as a register contains only `genocide`, so
  `has_register_core` duplicates `has_genocide`.

### 3.5 The reference dates

All 35 are accurately dated and sourced, and the loader's insistence on a primary-source
URL is good. Two reservations. Most `council` events are endogenous: resolutions adopted in
the very meetings that produce the speeches, so the overlay annotates the series with
itself. And the legal milestones a reader of this chart would want are absent: Akayesu
(2 September 1998, the first international genocide conviction), Krstić (2 August 2001),
the Darfur Commission's "no genocidal policy" report (S/2005/60), *Bosnia v. Serbia*
(26 February 2007), the first al-Bashir warrant (4 March 2009), Karadžić (2016), Mladić
(2017), and above all S/PV.7155 (16 April 2014), the corpus's largest meeting and the
probable cause of the 2014 spike. Keep the overlay as navigation, as `docs/VALIDATION.md`
says; add the legal milestones, which are the ones that make it analytically useful.

### 3.6 What a corpus linguist or computational social scientist would add

Ranked by value per unit of effort. Each answers a question the current figures cannot.

1. **A mixed model with a meeting random effect** (`has_genocide ~ s(year) + speaker_group
   + agenda + (1|meeting) + (1|speaker)`, `statsmodels` `BinomialBayesMixedGLM` or lme4
   via R). *Does the temporal signal survive clustering and composition?* One script,
   minutes on 106 k rows. It also delivers the delivery-language question `04` defers.
2. **Grammatical frames of the node**, as a regex pass over the concordance: *acts of
   genocide*, *crime of genocide*, *risk of genocide*, *amounts to / constitutes genocide*,
   *so-called genocide*, *genocide against X*, *genocidal intent*, plus the noun / adjective
   / perpetrator-noun split. *Nomination, hedging, denial or legal qualification, over time
   and by speaker.* Low cost; `08` already stores every occurrence, and the result
   triangulates the model's `stance` and `function` labels for free.
3. **Dispersion per term and collocate** (document frequency, distinct meetings, DP).
   *Is the word a property of a few debates or of the register?* Fifty lines over existing
   columns.
4. **Agenda-standardised rates** (direct standardisation to the corpus agenda mix). *How much
   of a year's rate is scheduling?* Trivial; reuses `breakdown()`.
5. **Passage embeddings of the 6,092 occurrences** (see §6): cluster, and report purity
   against `stance`, `function` and `referent` from both runs, with base rates. *Are the
   codebook's categories real in the distributional signal?* Medium; reuses `07`'s tested
   code, CPU only.
6. **Collocates over time with meeting-block bootstrap intervals and logDice** (roadmap S5).
   *Which associations are stable and which are one debate?* Medium.
7. **Usage-fluctuation analysis**: change points on the collocate profile rather than on
   frequency. *Did the word change meaning, or only frequency?* Medium; reuses
   `series.change_points`.
8. **Sequential uptake within meetings** (roadmap S6) from `speech_number`. Low cost.
9. **A synthetic control**: the ratio of `genocide` to `war_crimes + crimes_against_humanity`
   through the same change-point machinery. Trivial; partly present as `atrocity_core`.

Two things I would *not* add: generic sentiment, and a diachronic embedding drift of the
word, which would measure the referent mix (Rwanda 1994, Darfur 2004, Gaza 2023), not sense
change of a fixed legal term.

## 4. The model-assisted layer and the prompt

### 4.1 What the two runs say

Both runs cover all 6,092 occurrences with the byte-identical prompt. The figures below were
recomputed from the committed `annotations.jsonl` files and reproduce `docs/VALIDATION.md`
§7.

| Field | Luna (gpt-5.6-luna) | Gemini (3.7-flash) | Agreement | κ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| verdict | TP 6,086 · FP 6 | TP 6,092 | 99.9 % | degenerate |
| quotation | not_quoted 81 % · attributed 16 % · direct 3 % | 90 % · 8 % · 2 % | 90.2 % | 0.615 |
| stance | asserts 53 % · neutral-legal 23 % · attributes 12 % · hypothetical 9 % · rejects 1.7 % | 60 % · 28 % · 3.5 % · 7 % · 1.7 % | 81.2 % | 0.688 |
| referent | rwanda 28.5 % · general 21 % · bosnia 12 % · other 5.7 % · unclear 0.8 % | 30 % · 19 % · 12 % · 4.8 % · 0.07 % | 87.6 % | 0.853 |
| function | set equality | | 69.9 % | Jaccard 0.80 |
| confidence | high 92 % · medium 8 % · low 4 | high 99 % · medium 1 % | 91.5 % | 0.076 |

Read across, the layer is in better shape than "experimental" suggests for some fields and
worse for others:

- **Stable:** verdict (trivially), the named-case referents (per-class F1 0.95–0.97 for
  rwanda, bosnia, darfur, kosovo, burundi, south_sudan; 0.92 ukraine), `rejects_or_denies`
  (both runs find exactly 106; 78 shared out of a union of 134, F1 0.74), the
  `commemoration` and `institutional_title` functions (κ 0.83, 0.87).
- **Unstable:** `attributes_or_reports` (F1 0.37; 445 occurrences are Luna=attributes /
  Gemini=asserts, the single largest disagreement cell), the `attributed_or_reported`
  quotation label (539 rows Luna-only), `hypothetical_future` as a referent (F1 0.40),
  `drc_great_lakes` (0.61; 51 rows go DRC→Rwanda, the FDLR / ex-génocidaires problem),
  `unclear` (0.08), the accusation/accountability split in `function` (about 520 rows differ
  only on whether `accusation_or_qualification` accompanies `accountability`).
- **Abstention is near zero in both** (Luna 49 referent-unclear, Gemini 4; one stance-unclear
  each) and Gemini's confidence is uninformative (99 % high). Luna's `medium` is informative:
  referent agreement 0.67 against 0.90 for `high`.
- **Test–retest on the 91 pilot occurrences** (same model, same bytes): Luna stance 0.945,
  referent 0.945, all five fields identical 0.758; Gemini 0.989 / 0.989 / 0.912. About a
  quarter of Luna's own labels move between two calls. That is the noise floor any
  cross-model "contested" figure has to be read against, and it is not yet stated anywhere.
- **Anachronisms are a vocabulary gap, not hallucination.** Luna assigned `gaza` to 32
  occurrences before 2023 (17 in the January 2009 debate, 10 in 2014); Gemini assigned none
  and filed them under `other` as "gaza (2008-2009)" and "gaza 2014". Both assigned
  `nagorno_karabakh` six times before 2020 (Azerbaijan on Khojaly). No run assigned
  `isil_yazidi` before 2014, `myanmar_rohingya` before 2016 or `darfur` before 2003.
- **The `other` bucket says what `referents.csv` is missing:** Israel/Palestine before 2023
  (about 100–130 rows per model), ISIL crimes not against Yazidis (about 60), Syria (35 /
  18), Croatia and the Yugoslav wars (24 / 27), Bangladesh 1971 (14 / 14), Abkhazia and
  South Ossetia (about 10 / 13), Khojaly, apartheid South Africa, the Hazara, the Holodomor,
  "Muslims in India". Sri Lanka, Tigray and Guatemala do not appear.
- **Referent agreement rises with occurrences per speech** (0.83 at one occurrence, 0.93
  at ten or more) while function agreement falls; there is no positional abstention bias.

### 4.2 The prompt

`PROMPT.md` is a good instrument. The task boundary ("you classify discourse, you never
decide whether an event legally constitutes genocide") is exactly right, the fields mirror
the human codebook one-to-one so the two are commensurable, the verbatim-quote rule is
enforced mechanically and works (0.25 % and 0.05 % unlocated), and hashing the raw bytes
into every row is the right provenance discipline. The weaknesses are all in the category
definitions, and the disagreement data above points at each of them.

1. **Three fields encode the same distinction.** Stance `neutral_legal_reference`, function
   `institutional_title_or_mandate` and the meta referents `genocide_in_general`,
   `genocide_convention_law`, `institutional_mandate` all ask "is this a concrete case or a
   legal / abstract mention?". The largest referent confusion cells are precisely these
   (general ↔ convention_law 135, general → institutional_mandate 81). `hypothetical_future`
   is not a referent at all: 91 of Luna's 96 rows carrying it have stance
   `hypothetical_or_conditional`. The prompt gives no rule for choosing among the three meta
   referents.
2. **`attributes_or_reports` is undefined at its boundary with `asserts`.** "Reports a
   characterization without clear endorsement" leaves open whether "those convicted of
   genocide by the ICTR" is a report or an assertion. Luna reads it as report (753 rows),
   Gemini as assertion (215). On the 111 quotes containing *convicted / indicted for
   genocide*, stance agreement is 0.54. The same ambiguity drives the quotation field.
   "Quotation does not determine stance" is stated; how the two relate is not.
3. **No worked examples from the corpus.** Every example is invented. The corpus's own
   recurrent formulae are absent: "genocide against the Tutsi", "genocide denial / ideology",
   "génocidaires", "the Special Adviser on the Prevention of Genocide", the Rome triad
   "genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity", commemorative "we honour the victims
   of the genocide". Consequences: commemorations are coded `asserts` in 93 % of cases with
   no instruction saying so; "genocide denial" is `accusation` for Luna and `accountability`
   for Gemini.
4. **Whole-speech context helps the referent, but nothing records where the referent came
   from.** 25–30 % of `rwanda_1994` rows have no Rwanda cue in the evidence quote; for
   `kosovo`, `darfur` and `gaza` it is 40–47 %. The header line "Speaker (country or
   organisation)" is a shortcut the model may be taking; the only guard is "not the
   speaker's own country unless the passage applies the word to it".
5. **The referents table's year ranges are read as constraints by one model and as
   descriptions by the other** (the Gaza case above). The codebook already says the `years`
   column is documentation; the prompt renders it (`llm.py:281-282`) without saying so.
6. **The false-positive cascade over-reaches.** "All four fields, with no exception" led Luna
   to write the literal string `not_applicable` as the *evidence quote* for three of its six
   false positives, while the codebook requires an evidence span for a false positive.
   Prompt and codebook diverge, and `llm.py:456-484` does not check.
7. **Fields the study's own question needs are missing.** Nothing records who is accused,
   who the victim group is, whether the speaker's own state is the accused (the denial
   question Phase L exists for), or whether the use is a passing triad or a sustained
   argument. The triad is a large share of the corpus and is coded identically to a
   substantive accusation.
8. **Settings.** Neither request sets a temperature; both run provider defaults. Reasoning
   "high" costs 2.9 M reasoning tokens (Luna) and 9.7 M thinking tokens (Gemini, 1,589 per
   occurrence against 213 output tokens) with no ablation at medium. The rendered system
   message is about 2.8 k tokens, 4.2 k characters of it the referents table, sent 3,273
   times: about 9 M of Luna's 13.8 M input tokens are the fixed prefix. Both APIs offer
   prompt caching; neither script uses it.

**Prompt v2, concretely** (a new run id for both models):

- Split stance into `speaker_position` {asserts, rejects, conditional,
  reports_without_position} and move "legal / abstract" out of stance into a boolean
  `concrete_case`. Then `neutral_legal_reference`, `institutional_title_or_mandate` and the
  three meta referents collapse into one decision, and `hypothetical_future` disappears as
  a referent.
- Define `attributes_or_reports` positively: "use only when the passage names the
  characterising actor *and* the speaker neither adopts nor rejects it; a legal status the
  speaker treats as settled ('genocide convicts') is `asserts`".
- Add eight examples drawn from the corpus: one commemoration, one triad, one
  *génocidaires*, one "genocide ideology", one own-state denial, one tribunal report, one
  Convention reference, one hypothetical warning.
- Add `accused_actor` and `victim_group` (free text), `own_state_accused` {yes, no, n/a},
  `salience` {passing, substantive}, `referent_source` {passage, speech, header}, and a
  one-sentence `rationale`. The rationale makes disagreements diagnosable at almost no cost.
- Say that a false positive still needs a verbatim quote, and that `years` is documentation.
- Set the minimum quote as "the clause containing the modal or the attribution" (186 Luna
  quotes are under 40 characters, e.g. "have been convicted of genocide", which cannot
  support a stance label).
- Run a medium-effort ablation on the 50-speech pilot first; the retest figures give the
  noise floor to beat. Enable prompt caching.

### 4.4 The evaluation design

A 200-occurrence sample with two coders estimates *overall* κ per field well enough
(SE ≈ 0.05 for stance, 0.03 for referent) and will detect a gross failure. It cannot give
per-class precision and recall. Using the Luna marginals, an equal-probability draw of 200
contains about 3.5 `rejects_or_denies`, 18 `hypothetical_or_conditional`, 25
`attributes_or_reports` and 5 `direct_quotation`; 18 of the 29 referents have an expected
count below one. The coverage frame in `13_gold_sample.py` anchors one occurrence per
decade × cue stratum, which guarantees presence, not measurability, and the rejection cues
find rejection *language*, about half of which is not a rejection.

Now that two full runs exist, the sample design can use them:

- Keep the 195-occurrence probability + coverage sample as the unbiased estimate of overall
  accuracy; it already carries inclusion weights.
- Add a **disagreement-stratified frame** with recorded inclusion probabilities: all 134
  occurrences in the union of the two runs' `rejects_or_denies`; 100 of the 614
  `hypothetical_or_conditional`; 100 of the 789 `attributes_or_reports`; 100 of the 1,703
  contested on stance or referent; 60 of the 641 `other`; 40 with a pre-onset referent.
  About 530 more occurrences, 730 in all, and per-class recall for the rare stance classes
  becomes estimable at ±10–15 points. Because both models label everything, the adjudicated
  contested set also says which instrument erred, which L8 already promises.
- Report per-class F1 only where reference support ≥ 20; counts otherwise.
- Metrics: Cohen's κ *and* a prevalence-independent statistic (PABAK or Gwet's AC1), because
  verdict, quotation and confidence are dominated by one class; weighted rather than macro F1
  for referent; Krippendorff's α with the MASI distance plus per-label κ for `function`.
  Report the probability frame (weighted) and the contested frame (unweighted) separately.
- Schedule the third reader now rather than hoping for one, or adjudicate only where coders
  and models both disagree.

### 4.5 Code issues in 14, 15, 16 and `lib/usage.py`

1. **The manifest's `requests.submitted` accumulates across passes** (`14_llm_annotate.py:570`,
   `16_llm_annotate_gemini.py:838`): the Gemini manifest's 7,966 is 3,273 + 2,473 + 1,673 +
   546 + 1 over five quota-refused passes, not requests actually sent; `returned: 4474`
   counts re-downloads. `15_usage.py:524` publishes `submitted` as the view's "Requests"
   figure, so promoting the Gemini run would print 7,966. Count requests at job creation and
   returns per new `custom_id`; record a list of passes with their mode.
2. **14 lacks the duplicate-row guard 16 gained** after commit `2fbd205`. A `--poll` on a
   run whose earlier jobs were already harvested would double rows, and `usage.row_problems`
   would then refuse the run rather than repair it. Port the `already` parameter.
3. **`cost_usd` is null by design** while the manifest holds exact token counts and the
   roadmap records "~$12" in prose. Record the price table (URL, date, rates, batch
   discount) in the manifest and compute the figure.
4. **The evidence locator is exact-or-whitespace only** (`llm.py:599-634`). Seven of Luna's
   15 unlocated quotes are plausible curly-quote / dash / OCR-whitespace mismatches. Add an
   NFKC + quote/dash-folding fallback flagged as `evidence_relocated`, and require a
   located, non-empty quote for false positives.
5. **`cohens_kappa` returns 0.000 for verdict** (p_e = 0.998) and the artefact carries the
   bare number. Withhold κ below a minority-class share of 1 %, or publish PABAK beside it.
6. **`reference_labels` scores the model only on fields where the two coders agreed**
   (`usage.py:733-768`), which is the easy subset, with n differing per field. The artefact
   must carry the excluded share per field; after adjudication, score against adjudicated
   labels only.
7. **`function_jaccard` compares the coders' pipe strings by string equality**
   (`usage.py:867`), so `a|b` versus `b|a` is silently dropped, while the model comparison
   uses set equality (`:972-984`). Use `_multi` on both.
8. **`classification` averages macro-F1 over the union of classes** (`usage.py:655-689`),
   so every label a model used that the reference never did contributes F1 = 0; with 29
   referent classes and n ≈ 200 the macro figure is dominated by empty classes.
9. **Thinking tokens count against `max_output_tokens` on Gemini**, so the shared ceiling

   tokens per occurrence; that is the recorded `MAX_TOKENS` failure.
10. `gather`, `write_manifest` and `refuse_mismatch` are duplicated between 14 and 16; the
    parity test pins constants, not behaviour. Lift them into `lib`.
11. Minor: `MINIMUM_OCCURRENCES = 20` (`usage.py:71`) does not guard a 1.7 % base-rate
    share (expected count at n = 20 is 0.35), so `share_rejects` is a ranking of noise; all
    four manifests carry a `-dirty` commit.

## 6. Non-adopted methods, infrastructure and tests

### 6.1 Embeddings (06): right machinery, wrong unit

The chunking and token-weighted pooling policy is sound but applies to nine speeches out of
106,302. Two real defects: only speeches whose space count × 2 exceeds the window are
tokenised exactly (`lib/embeddings.py:155-157`), so any other speech that overshoots is
silently truncated by the model, and `index.parquet.encoded_tokens` holds a space count for
almost every row (`06_embed.py:302-306`). The registry pins `revision: main`
(`config/embedding_models.yml:39,59,74`) and the manifest records that string, so a 06
artefact cannot name the weights that produced it. Pin the Hugging Face commit hash.

More important, a whole-speech vector encodes the occasion (06's own inspection: nearest
neighbour is the same delegation 55 % of the time). For a study of one word the unit is the
occurrence, and the project already enumerates 6,092 of them with stable IDs, a ±150-character
window and the containing sentence. Encoding those passages is a CPU job (minutes with
`granite-small-en`, under an hour with Qwen3-0.6B) and produces a 6,092 × 1,024 matrix that
can be archived. Uses, cheapest and most defensible first: (1) cluster the passages with
the 07 machinery and report purity / ARI against `stance`, `function` and `referent` from
both LLM runs, with base rates, as independent evidence that the codebook's categories are
real; (2) a "similar passages" affordance (k = 10) in the concordance, caveated as
navigation; (3) a UMAP map coloured by referent, in the reader, never as a figure.

### 6.2 Topic models (07): the deferral is right, for a stronger reason than stated

The evaluation battery (null-calibrated abstention, equal-abstention re-reading,
bootstrap + seed ARI, NPMI, a properly blinded intrusion task, a projection built to argue
against itself) is more careful than most published topic-model work. Four gaps: only NMF's
k is swept, never HDBSCAN's `min_cluster_size` or UMAP's `n_neighbors`
(`lib/topics.py:483-485`), so the "62, 61, 64, 66, 62 topics" instability may be a
hyperparameter artefact; ARI counts the noise set as a class (`:534-540`); NMF is labelled
by c-TF-IDF over argmax documents, not by its own factors (`:438`), so the intrusion task
tests c-TF-IDF labels; and the verdict thresholds 0.4 / 0.7 / 0.05 live in `build_verdict`
(`07_topics.py:707-731`), written after the first run. The decisive finding is that 74 % of
a point's 25 nearest neighbours share its hand-coded agenda item: a speech-level topic model
re-derives a column the corpus already has. Any reopening should be at passage level (6.1),
not by rerunning 07 with another k.

### 6.3 Lemma layer (10): keep as a sensitivity reading

The alignment logic is correct and conservative; no bug found. Three limits to record:
`TOKEN_RE` is ASCII-only, so `Türkiye` fragments in both vocabularies; possessives never
collapse; and `12_speaker_keyness.py` has no lemma path, so adopting lemmas for 05 would put
the two keyness tables on different vocabularies.

### 6.4 Reproducibility: strong chain, missing archive

The pinned MD5s verified offline, the hashed lock, the manifests, the `analysis_hash` and
the export-seam contract are exemplary. What is missing: a tagged release with the derived
payload and manifests deposited on Zenodo (today they exist only in an Actions cache that
evicts after seven idle days, with a WAF-challenged Dataverse as backstop); a DAG (the step
order is hand-copied in `deploy.yml`, `scripts/README.md` and `docs/CLUSTER.md`, and 10
feeds 05 out of numerical order; a small Snakefile or Makefile with sentinel manifests
would make the graph the single source of truth); a container or `pip freeze` for the
cluster environment (`setup_env.sh:49-51` says "report, rather than enforce" while
`requirements-cluster.txt:15-16` says "asserts"); and a check that the lock is still in
sync with the ranges in `requirements.txt`.

### 6.5 Tests and CI

`pytest` and `ruff` pass here on a fresh Python 3.11 venv, and `checks.yml` runs both suites
plus two Playwright configurations. The gaps: no test executes a numbered script end to end
(a synthetic corpus through 01 → `export_web.py` with golden JSON would catch a silent
arithmetic change, which the value-blind contract cannot); the heavy paths (`fit_nmf`,
`fit_embedding`, `lemmatise`, `07_topics.stability`, `build_verdict`) are never run outside
the cluster (a weekly optional job on `requirements-cluster.txt` at `--limit 200` on CPU
would catch the failure class `submit_topics.sh:42-49` describes); several tests assert on
source text (`test_cluster.py:188-194, 250-257`, `test_topics.py:777-845`) and would break
under `ruff format`; `filterwarnings = error::DeprecationWarning` will fail the first lock
regeneration; no Dependabot.

### 6.6 Code quality

Module boundaries are good and the libraries are genuinely reusable. The orchestrators are
long because they carry prose: `15_usage.py:612-1023` (about 410 lines of note text),
`07_topics.py:406-691`, `04_series.py:573-835`, `05_lexical.py:441-594`. Moving note
rendering into `lib/notes/` would halve them. Duplication: `MATCH_ON` and `PERIODS` defined
twice (05 and `lib/keyness.py`, 05 and `lib/actors.py`); the 20-seed stability loop in 05
re-implements `keyness._stability`; `write_csv` three times; `stability`, `formulaic_terms`
and `model_payload` in 07 are `lib` material and untested because they are not there.
`lib/topics.py` (1,365 lines) has four concerns and should be a package.

## 7. Documentation

The documentation is the project's greatest strength and its heaviest cost. Every decision

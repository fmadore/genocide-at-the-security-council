# Figure and page text: proposed revision — 19 October 2026

A proposal for review. Nothing here has been applied to the site.

## Purpose

The figure texts written in September explain how to read each chart and what it
cannot show. They seldom say why a reader interested in genocide should look at the
chart at all. This revision rewrites the two places where that reason belongs: each
figure's **question** and each page's **introduction**. Reading notes and caveats
keep their content; a short list of copy-editing fixes follows at the end.

The revision draws on the questions raised in the draft article for the *Journal of
Genocide Research* (Glasman, Madore and Schönfeld, draft of 5 October 2026), without
reusing its wording.

## Rules followed

- **Ask, do not assert.** The draft proposes five uses of the word: mobilisation,
  adherence, scandalisation, refutation and avoidance. No figure is named after a
  use, and no question implies one. The classifier codes position, referent and
  function (accusation, prevention, commemoration, accountability), not the five
  uses, and its labels are still unvalidated (0 of 469 gold rows coded, see
  [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md)).
- **Non-use is not avoidance.** A speech without the word is a fact about the
  record. No question suggests that a delegation held the word back on purpose.
- **Order is not influence.** A later first use is later; it does not show that a
  delegation followed another.
- **Each question must be one the figure can answer.** Where the figure only counts,
  the question asks about counts.
- **Budgets unchanged**: question ≤ 20 words, reading ≤ 60, caveat ≤ 50.
- **British English**, plain words, no undefined jargon in a question.
- Utility figures (reading-set summaries, the model comparison) keep their questions.

### Overview

**Current.** Between {first year} and {last year}, the UN Security Council records
contain {speeches} speeches from {meetings} meetings. Of these, {bearing}
({share}) mention *genocide* or related forms, such as *genocidal*. Explore when the
word appears, who uses it and what the surrounding passages say.

**Proposed.** Between {first year} and {last year}, the UN Security Council records
hold {speeches} speeches from {meetings} meetings. Only {bearing} of them ({share})
mention *genocide* or a related form such as *genocidal*. Trace when the word was
said, by whom and about which situations, and whether speakers asserted the label or
rejected it. Every count leads back to the passages behind it.

*Why:* opens on the rarity of the word, the starting point of the article, and names
the four questions the site can answer.

### Chronology

**Current.** Compare term use across {n} years. Counts show how many mentions or
speeches appear in the record. Rates account for the changing volume of speech:
choose a share of speeches or occurrences per 100,000 words.

**Proposed.** Follow when the Council reached for *genocide* and the words around it,
across {n} years. Counts show how many mentions or speeches the record holds; rates
allow for the growing volume of speech. Choose a share of speeches or occurrences per
100,000 words.

*Why:* says what the page is for before saying how to set it.

### Words in context (Language)

**Current, first paragraph.** Examine the phrases around *genocide*, the words
commonly used nearby and the terms found in the same speech. Compare periods and
speakers, then follow a word to its passages to assess how it was used.

**Proposed, first paragraph.** The word reaches the Council both in legal formulas,

it, the words used nearby and the terms found in the same speech. Compare periods
and speakers, then follow a word to its passages.

*Second paragraph* (collocate and keyness definitions): keep.

*Why:* gives the reader a reason to look at phrasing at all.

### Actors

### Usage

**Current.** Examine which events or concepts speakers associate with *genocide* and

**Proposed.** One word can make a claim, refuse one or report someone else's.

*Why:* states the reason the page exists, which a keyword count cannot capture. The

## Added caveat: the 2020–2021 gap

The corpus holds no speeches for April–June 2020 or January–April 2021, when the
Council met by videoconference and kept no verbatim records. The site does not say
so, and the trough reads as a fall in activity. Proposed additions:

| Figure | Slot | Added sentence |
|---|---|---|

## Notes for the article

These came up while matching the figures to the draft. None is a site change.

1. **The figures do not test the five uses.** They show where to look: "Who stays
   silent" for joining in or not, "When each delegation first said it" for the order
   of first uses, "Who held a seat when they spoke" for the speaker's position, "Who
   rejects the word" for refusals. Testing the typology needs its own coding: written
   criteria, independent readers, meeting context, and more than one use allowed per
   passage. The classifier's categories (position, referent, function) are a
   different scheme.
2. **Non-use needs its own denominator.** Most speeches without the word concern
   other subjects or procedure. The Chronology's *atrocity vocabulary without the
   word* set (speeches using *ethnic cleansing*, *crimes against humanity* or *war
   crimes* and no *genocid\** match) is a better starting point than the 97.5%, but
   it still shows non-use, not avoidance.
3. **Commemoration is already a model function.** The classifier labels
   commemorative passages, and the lexicon has a commemorative register. It has no
   place among the five uses yet.
4. **The Germany claim.** Germany's records begin in 1991. Over the common period,
   1991–2024, its share of speeches using the word (1.92%) is below the United
   States (2.80%) and France (2.46%), but not "far less". China's share over the
   same years is 0.50%, so the resemblance to Chinese usage does not hold either.
   The counts say nothing about party incentives. Figures recomputed from
   `web/static/data/actor_year/actor_year.csv` on 19 October 2026.
5. **Counts.** The current data give 7,787 occurrences in 4,136 of 167,642 speeches
   (2.47%), 1946–2024. The draft's 7,747 and 4,130 come from an earlier pattern, and
   the draft's end year of 2025 is wrong. Freeze the figures at a release before
   submission.
6. **Source errors.** Two errors an external review reported are confirmed against
   the UN PDFs and logged in `docs/VALIDATION.md`
   ([PR #39](https://github.com/fmadore/genocide-at-the-security-council/pull/39)).
   Neither is corrected yet.
   - In S/PV.1379 (16 November 1967), Mali's statement and its genocide passage are
     filed under Israel. Israel's first Holocaust mention on the site would move to
     March 1968.
   - The record of S/PV.903 (15 September 1960) is copied under S/PV.869 (28 June
     1960). This makes Ecuador the first delegation to call the Congo a genocide,
     two days before independence. The real first is in September 1960.

   First-use and country claims in the article need a check against the original
   record.

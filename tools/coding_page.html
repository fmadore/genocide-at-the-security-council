Annotating the word “genocide” in Security Council speeches

You will read passages from speeches at the UN Security Council. In each one the word “genocide” is highlighted. Your job is to describe how the speaker uses the word. You are never asked whether a genocide really took place.

For each passage

  1. Read the highlighted paragraph. Scroll up or down in the speech if you need more context.
  2. Answer the questions on the right. Every option comes with a short explanation or an example.
  3. Explain your reading in one sentence, and click Done.

Five rules to keep in mind

  • Describe, do not judge. Record what the speaker says, whatever you think of it.
  • Look at the highlighted word only. A speech may discuss a real massacre while the highlighted word sits in a treaty’s name.
  • Keeping the word at a distance is rejecting it. “So-called genocide”, “allegations of genocide” and scare quotes count as rejecting the label.
  • Remembering is asserting. A commemoration (“we honour the victims of the genocide”) treats the genocide as a fact.
  • When unsure, say so. Choose “cannot tell” or low confidence instead of guessing, and leave a note for your supervisor.
